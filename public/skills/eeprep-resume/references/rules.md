# Layout and section rules

From [eeprep's resume page](https://eeprep.com/advice/resume/), which condenses the r/EngineeringResumes wiki. Where a rule is the eeprep author's and not the wiki's, it says so.

## Layout

- One page. The wiki allows a second page at about 10 years of experience.
- One column. No icons, images, graphics, photo, logos, tables, or text boxes.
- Black text. Line spacing of at least 1.07.
- No justified text. No hand indents.
- Every date right-aligned to the right margin.
- Bold only on section titles, entry titles, and the coursework, activities, and skills labels. Never on a keyword inside a bullet.
- Author's rule: 10 to 11 pt text and 0.5 in margins. The wiki asks for 10.5 pt or larger, and eeprep allows 10 pt so a full page still fits.
- Author's rule: center the header, left-align everything below it.
- Fill the page without cramming it. Empty gaps and half-used lines are wasted space. Never shrink white space to fit one more line.
- The same gap between every pair of sections and every pair of entries.

## Section order

| Who                                           | Order                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Student with no technical job yet             | Education, Skills, Projects, Experience                   |
| Student whose projects are the strongest part | Projects, Experience, Education, Skills (author's advice) |
| Graduate at work                              | Experience, Education, Skills                             |
| Working engineer                              | Experience, Projects, Education, Skills (author's advice) |

Skills goes right under Education in every order.

Cut the objective or summary. The wiki keeps one only for senior engineers, career changers, and people returning from a gap. If the user insists, it is one line that names the role: "Seeking a summer 2027 embedded firmware internship."

## Header

Name centered in large type, one contact line under it, no rule line between them. Separate items with a vertical bar. No icons and no labels such as "Phone:". Links as plain text, such as linkedin.com/in/alex-doe-example. The wiki calls a phone number and LinkedIn unnecessary, but the author lists both. Every link must work on the day the page goes out.

Put "Willing to relocate" next to the location if it is true. A city far from the job can count against the candidate.

## Education

Degree on the first line with GPA on the same line. School under it with the graduation date at the right margin, "Expected" in front of it before graduation. One line each for "Relevant coursework:" and "Activities:", labels in bold. If you have taken graduate courses, list them first on their own line, labeled "Graduate coursework:". Write course names, such as Digital Signal Processing, never course codes. A code means nothing to a reader from another school. Drop high school after the first year. With a master's, keep the bachelor's line.

Coursework is where EE parts from the wiki. The wiki says to leave it off unless the courses are specialized. ECE interviewers say the courses show whether the student can pass the interview, and recruiters like it. List upper-level electives that match the posting.

GPA, author's rule of thumb: list 3.50 or higher. Michigan's and Berkeley's career centers use 3.0, so say this rule is stricter. If the posting asks for a GPA, list it. From 3.0 to 3.49, list it when the posting sets a minimum the user meets, and leave it off otherwise. Below 3.0, leave it off. If listed, match the decimals, as in 3.30/4.00.

## Projects

Call the section Projects. Leave the word "project" out of each title. Tools in parentheses after the title, dates at the right margin. Pick one date style, semesters or months, and use it on every line of the section. Sections may differ, such as semesters for projects and months for jobs (author's advice). Give the best projects more bullets and cut weak ones. Give firmware its own bullets, and for FPGA work, the testbenches. Move real technical club work out of Activities and into its own Projects entry.

## Experience

Call it Experience. Engineering roles get a bold title and company with dates at the right margin. The location goes on the next line, under the date at the right margin, then the bullets. A job outside engineering depends on volume. With good engineering experience, it gets one line with no bullets, or goes. Without that, it gets bullets, using what the user can.

## Skills

Two or three comma-separated lines with a bold label, most important first:

- Software: KiCad, LTspice, C, Python
- Hardware/Lab: oscilloscope, electronic load, logic analyzer, soldering
- Certifications: only ones the user can prove

Cut soft skills. Leave IDEs, editors, and notebook environments such as VS Code and Jupyter out of Skills, and name the tool in the bullet where it produced a result. Cut a vague "Knowledge" line unless it is certifiable. Write exact product names: C, C++ as two items, Ansys HFSS with the company name. When a posting names a skill the user has, use the posting's word. Every skill needs a bullet behind it. One line per group.

## A CV for a graduate application

When the user applies to a master's or PhD program, they may need a CV, not a resume. The other rules still apply, with these differences:

- Length: up to two pages.
- Page break: do not split an entry's heading from its bullets across the page break.
- Order: Education first, Skills right under it, then Experience.
- Research: add research, papers, and talks.
- GPA: list the overall GPA with its scale, such as 3.44/4.00, even below the 3.50 cutoff a job resume uses.
- Graduate GPA: do not put a graduate-only GPA on the degree line in place of the overall GPA.
- Teaching: grading, tutoring, or peer mentoring can sit under its own Teaching heading. Say how many students and how many classes.
- Type: 10.75 pt text is a good size for a two-page CV, 10.5 pt the smallest, with 0.5 in margins. These numbers are for the CV only. A job resume stays one page.

## Dates

One style inside each section. "Present" for an ongoing role. Spell month names: Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, June, July, Aug, Sept, Oct, Nov, Dec. No days, no abbreviated years.

## Questions students ask

- Missing some requirements? Apply anyway for tools and years. The degree is different: a required degree is usually a hard filter.
- A senior design project that did not work? List it. Describe the user's part with verbs such as designed and tested, and give the numbers they did reach.
- Interests? Only specific ones. If the user cannot name specifics, delete the section.
- Cover letter? Write one when the resume is not an obvious fit. Big tech recruiters often skip them, but defense contractors and Midwest firms tend to read them. Two short paragraphs on why the user meets the posting.

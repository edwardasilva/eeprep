# Inventory: get the raw material first

Before you touch a layout, get the user to list everything. Most students leave half their material in their heads. Ask in one message, then fill the gaps with short follow-ups.

## What to ask for

1. Every class lab or course project where they built, wrote, or measured something. Course number, what it did, what they measured, and with what instrument.
2. Every club, design team, or competition build, with the part they owned.
3. Every repair, side project, or thing they built at home, finished or not. An unfinished project with real progress still counts.
4. Every research seat, with the professor's field and what they produced.
5. Every job, engineering or not. Dates, title, employer, and one line on what it was.
6. Upper-level courses that match the jobs they want.
7. Tools they can use without help: software, languages, lab instruments.
8. Certifications they can prove.
9. The role they want next, in one line, such as "summer 2027 embedded firmware internship". Every later choice serves that line.
10. GPA, and whether the postings they want set a minimum.

## Questions that turn a task into a result

Ask these for each project or role. Never answer them yourself.

- What did you build or change? Team or solo? How big was the team, and which part was yours?
- How did you do it, and why that way? Which tools, standards, or references did you use?
- Did you test it? What did you measure, with what instrument, and what number came out?
- What broke, and how did you find it?
- Did it meet the goal? By how much did it beat or miss the spec?
- What would you change next time?

## What to do with the answers

- A build with a measured number becomes a project entry with one to three bullets.
- A build with no number yet gets a bullet with `[X]` where the number goes, and a note to the user to measure it before they send the page.
- Once the page has engineering work, a job outside engineering gets one line with no bullets. It shows the person can hold a job. Before that, it can keep bullets.
- A real design-team role goes in Projects, with bullets, not in an Activities line.
- A course goes in one Relevant coursework line under Education, only if it matches the posting.

## Alex Doe

eeprep's running example is Alex Doe, a made-up third-year EE student. Alex's inventory came to two builds (a CubeSat power board and a bench data logger), a retail job, three upper-level courses, and two clubs. That is enough for one page. If the user has less, the page has room at the bottom, and the fix is content, never tighter spacing.

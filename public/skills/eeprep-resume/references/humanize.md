# Make it read like a person wrote it

Hiring managers read hundreds of resumes and are reading for sameness. A page that reads like every other AI-drafted page is invisible at best. Run this pass over every bullet after the content is right. The rules adapt Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" and eeprep's style guide.

## Tells to remove

1. Inflated language. "Spearheaded a transformative initiative that revolutionized the workflow" for a script that saved 20 minutes a day. State the action and the result, and let the reader judge.
2. Tacked-on editorializing. "...reducing build time by 40%, streamlining the development process." The second clause adds nothing. Cut it.
3. A closing bullet that restates the role in vaguer words than the bullets above it. Every bullet adds a fact.
4. Transitions. "Moreover", "furthermore", "additionally" never belong in a bullet. If one appears, two bullets got merged into an essay sentence.
5. The "not just X, but Y" frame. State what was done.
6. Title Case In Section Headers. Section names are the standard short ones: Education, Projects, Experience, Skills.
7. Uniform rhythm. Every bullet the same length and shape reads as machine-made. Vary length and structure across a section.
8. Generic claims. "Collaborated with cross-functional teams to deliver high-quality solutions" fits any resume. A good bullet could not be pasted onto a different person's page without becoming false: named tools, real numbers, the constraint that made it hard.
9. Bolded lead-in labels. "Scalability: the system scales." Never bold a word inside a bullet.
10. Em dashes and en dashes doing a comma's job. Use a comma or split the sentence.
11. "Not only... but also." Split it into two plain bullets or state the stronger one.
12. Smoothed facts. If the user said "AVX-512 vectorization gave a 1.6x speedup on legacy DSP code", do not hand back "optimized software to improve performance". Put the specifics back.

## Words to cut on sight

leverage, robust, seamless, comprehensive, cutting-edge, innovative, dynamic, passionate, results-driven, synergy, utilize, spearhead, showcase, foster, bolster, enhance, pivotal, crucial, meticulous, in order to.

## How to apply it

- Read each bullet against the twelve tells one at a time. They are easy to miss when you skim for "does this sound bad".
- Where a bullet fails, put the missing specific back. Ask the user for it if it is not in the conversation. Never invent it.
- Read the whole section aloud. If a real engineer would not say a line that way in an interview, rewrite it.
- Matching one tell does not prove a bullet is machine-written, and over-correcting makes stilted prose. The goal is a page that reads like one specific person describing specific work.

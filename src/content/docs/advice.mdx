Start with the [[Quick setup|quick setup page]], the whole guide on one page in seven steps. Then read the four sections in order. Projects give a resume something to show, and the resume starts every application. The [[Author's story|author's story page]] says where the advice comes from.

<MapFigure />

The advice on these pages is one engineer's opinion, not a set of rules. If you know a better way, use it. To suggest a change, see the [[Contributing|contributing page]].

- The [[Projects|projects page]] explains how to pick one build you can defend and present it.
  - The [[Software|software page]] covers embedded firmware, test automation, FPGA and HDL work, and how to show it on GitHub.
  - The [[Hardware|hardware page]] covers bench skills, hardware builds, design teams, and technician roles.
  - The [[Career paths|career paths page]] covers five paths: embedded, power, analog, RF, and controls.
  - The [[Portfolio|portfolio page]] covers a project site that shows the work behind the bullets, and a LinkedIn profile that recruiters find.
- The [[Resume|resume page]] covers a one-page layout, section order, and bullets with measured results.
  - The [[Walkthrough|walkthrough page]] takes one student's resume from a first draft to a finished PDF, and shows how the page changes once experience takes over.
  - The [[Cleanup|cleanup page]] is the audit to run on the PDF before you send it.
  - The [[Workshop|workshop page]] is a timed resume workshop for a class or student group, with a script and handouts.
- The [[Getting hired|getting hired page]] covers referrals, the rule of 20 (a rule of thumb), career fairs, and where to look.
  - The [[Networking|networking page]] covers who to ask for a referral and how to ask.
  - The [[Job market|job market page]] covers the entry-level market now and how to adjust to it.
  - The [[How to interview|how to interview page]] covers project questions, fundamentals, coding, behavioral stories, and the on-site day.
  - The [[First internship|first internship page]] shows how to land a first internship with no experience.
  - The [[First full-time role|first full-time role page]] covers senior-year recruiting, return offers, and new-grad programs.
- The [[Forever learning|forever learning page]] covers datasheets and errata, free courses, the FE exam and PE license, and employer tuition help.
  - The [[Getting into graduate study|getting into graduate study page]] is the path to get ready for a master's or PhD.
  - The [[Graduate study|graduate study page]] covers when a master's or PhD pays off for an EE, and how students pay for one.
  - The [[Professional societies|professional societies page]] covers what IEEE, the honor societies, and SWE, NSBE, and SHPE give you.

Under the [[Help|help page]], the [[Job boards|job boards page]] says where each board misleads, the [[Resources|resources page]] lists the repos and lists this site leans on, and the [[AI use|AI use page]] covers where an AI tool helps in the search and where it costs you.

Start with the [[Quick setup|quick setup page]], the whole guide on one page in seven steps. Then read the four sections in order. Projects come first because they give a resume something to show, and the resume starts every application. The [[Author's story|author's story page]] says where the advice comes from.

<MapFigure />

The guide is written for EECS students and new graduates looking for an internship or a first job in the US. Its advice is one engineer's opinion, not a set of rules. If you know a better way, use it. To suggest a change, see the [[Contributing|contributing page]].

- The [[Projects|projects page]] explains how to pick one build you can defend, and how to present it.
  - The [[Software|software page]] covers embedded firmware, test automation, FPGA and HDL work, and how to show it on GitHub.
  - The [[Hardware|hardware page]] covers bench skills, hardware builds, design teams, and technician roles.
  - The [[Portfolio|portfolio page]] covers a project site that shows the work behind the bullets, and a LinkedIn profile that recruiters find.
- The [[Resume|resume page]] covers a one-page layout, section order, and bullets with measured results.
  - The [[Walkthrough|walkthrough page]] takes one student's resume from a first draft to a finished PDF, and shows how the page changes once experience takes over.
  - The [[Cleanup|cleanup page]] is the audit to run on the PDF before you send it.
  - The [[Workshop|workshop page]] is a timed resume workshop for a class or student group, with a script and handouts.
- The [[Getting hired|getting hired page]] covers referrals, the rule of 50 (a rule of thumb for when to reevaluate), career fairs, and where to look.
  - The [[Networking|networking page]] covers who to ask for a referral and how to ask.
  - The [[Startups|startups page]] covers finding small startups, writing to the founder after you apply, and the risks of a startup role.
  - The [[Job market|job market page]] covers the entry-level market now and how to adjust to it.
  - The [[Career paths|career paths page]] covers eleven paths, such as embedded, power, digital design, and verification. It also covers how to test one before you commit.
  - The [[How to interview|how to interview page]] covers project questions, fundamentals, coding, behavioral stories, and the on-site day.
  - The [[First internship|first internship page]] shows how to land a first internship with no experience.
  - The [[First full-time role|first full-time role page]] covers senior-year recruiting, return offers, and new-grad programs.
  - The [[Non-technical skills|non-technical skills page]] covers teamwork, communication, project management, a job outside engineering, and the habits an intern is judged on.
- The [[Forever learning|forever learning page]] covers datasheets and errata, free courses, the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam and the professional engineer (PE) license, and employer tuition help.
  - The [[Graduate study|graduate study page]] covers when a master's or PhD pays off for an electrical engineer, how students pay, and how to get ready and apply.
  - The [[Professional societies|professional societies page]] covers what IEEE, the honor societies, the Society of Women Engineers (SWE), the National Society of Black Engineers (NSBE), the Society of Hispanic Professional Engineers (SHPE), and a few others give you.

Under the [[Help|help page]], the [[AI use|AI use page]] covers where an AI tool helps in the search and where it costs you. The [[Job boards|job boards page]] says where each board misleads. The [[Resources|resources page]] collects the repositories and lists that this site leans on, and the [[Sources|sources page]] lists every outside link.

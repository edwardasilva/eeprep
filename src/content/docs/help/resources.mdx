These are the repositories and lists this site leans on. Each one is free, and none of them pays eeprep. Use a list to find the role, then apply on the employer's page.

## Job lists on GitHub

- [SimplifyJobs's internship list](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/Summer2027-Internships) is updated daily by Simplify and Pitt CSC. On November 9, 2026, it held 1,612 roles, 282 of them hardware. Closed roles get a lock and move to an inactive page.
- [SimplifyJobs's new-grad list](https://github.com/SimplifyJobs/New-Grad-Positions) is the same format for full-time roles. It held 601 roles that day, 119 of them hardware. The [[Job boards|job boards page]] says how these lists get their data.

## Resumes

- [The r/EngineeringResumes wiki](https://www.reddit.com/r/EngineeringResumes/wiki/index/) is the source for the rules on the [[Resume|resume page]]. Read the whole thing once.
- [Its template page](https://www.reddit.com/r/EngineeringResumes/wiki/templates/) has a Google Docs template and a LaTeX one.
- [resume-generator](https://github.com/edwardasilva/resume-generator) turns one JSON file into a one-page PDF with the layout from the resume page. The [[Walkthrough|walkthrough page]] shows the five example files it ships with.
- [Georgia Tech's 2026 cover letter guide](https://c14750.wpmucdn.com/5568/files/2026/10/Cover-Letter-Guide26.pdf) explains when to send a cover letter and how to write one, with undergraduate examples.

## Courses and references

- [MIT OpenCourseWare](https://ocw.mit.edu/about/) publishes materials from more than 2,500 courses. The [[Forever learning#Take free courses|section on taking free courses]] on the forever learning page names the three that cover the core of an EE degree.
- [NCEES](https://ncees.org/licensure/) lists the steps to a professional engineer license, including the Fundamentals of Engineering (FE) exam and the Principles and Practice of Engineering (PE) exam. The [[Forever learning#The FE exam and the PE license|section on the exams and the license]] on the forever learning page has the path.
- [O\*NET's electrical engineering page](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/17-2071.00) lists the job titles reported for the field. Search postings by those titles, not by "electrical engineer".

## Pay

- [The Bureau of Labor Statistics page on electrical and electronics engineers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/electrical-and-electronics-engineers.htm) has the federal numbers. It lists median pay, the bottom and top 10 percent, pay by industry, and projected job growth. The figures cover every career stage, so do not expect the median in an entry-level offer.
- [Levels.fyi](https://www.levels.fyi/) shows pay by company, level, and location. Use it to compare an offer with what others at the same company and level reported. Most of the data is self-reported, and many entries are software roles. Treat the numbers as ranges, and trust them less for small firms with few submissions.
- [The National Association of Colleges and Employers (NACE)](https://www.naceweb.org/job-market/compensation/) has free yearly summaries of its surveys on new-grad starting salaries and intern hourly pay. NACE groups data by broad discipline, so its engineering numbers include mechanical and civil as well as electrical. The free summaries are enough, so skip the paid member reports.

## Company lists

The [[Company list|company list page]] is a searchable list of companies by region, built from [company-lists](https://github.com/edwardasilva/company-lists). It covers Denver and Boulder (Colorado), Austin and Houston (Texas), Los Angeles and the San Francisco Bay Area (California), and Northern Virginia. Every row in the source links the public page it came from. Use it to start your own list, then check each company's career page and note the date you checked.

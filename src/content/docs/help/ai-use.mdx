Use AI to find work and to check your work. Do not let it write your resume, your application answers, or your interview answers. Interviewers go layers deep on every line, and a line you did not write is a line you cannot defend. A tool can build you a job-posting scraper. It should not write a line of your resume.

## Where a tool helps

A scraper that checks the career pages you follow and flags each new role is the best use. Read each posting yourself and apply by hand on the company's site.

Take-home tests and whiteboard interviews often allow no AI tools at all. The [[How to interview#Take-home tests|Take-home tests]] section on the How to interview page covers how to handle that.

## Finding postings

This is the best use. Ask the tool to write a script that checks the career pages of your top companies every day and tells you what is new. Ask it to sort a daily list by the titles you want: test, validation, controls, firmware. Then read each posting yourself and apply on the company's own site. Applying early matters, because [most recruiters review applications in the order they arrive](https://my.greenhouse.com/blogs/what-really-happens-after-you-apply-for-a-job). The [[Getting hired#Where to look|Where to look]] section on the Getting hired page lists the pages to watch.

## Checking a draft

Paste your resume and the posting, and ask for the gaps, not for new bullets. [UC Davis's career office suggests asking the tool to review your resume "like an expert coach"](https://alumni.ucdavis.edu/node/12666) and name five specific changes. Treat the answer as a list of places to look, not as text to paste in.

Reject any suggestion that adds a tool, a number, or a result you do not have. A keyword you cannot back up in an interview is a fabrication, and [a recruiter on X says candidates who padded their resumes with AI got caught in later rounds](https://x.com/randomrecruiter/status/2081829972415578607). The tool cannot measure your ripple or your efficiency. Every [X] on this site is a number you read off an instrument.

A chatbot also smooths everything into the same shape. If three suggestions start with the same verb, you are looking at the pattern, not at your work. Keep the specific version.

If you draft with a coding agent such as Claude Code or Cursor, give it the rules before it writes. [The eeprep resume skill](/skills/eeprep-resume/README.md) is a folder of Markdown files the agent reads first. It holds the layout rules, the bullet pattern, the tailoring steps, and the audit, and it tells the agent to write [X] where a number belongs instead of making one up. The [[Resume#Teach your AI the rules|Teach your AI the rules]] section on the Resume page has the install steps.

## Writing it yourself

Hiring managers are reading for sameness. In [a May 2025 survey of 600 US hiring managers by TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/ai-in-hiring-survey), about one in five said they would reject an application that appears fully AI-generated. The same managers found AI use least acceptable in skills assessments, at 40.8%, then in resume writing, at 30.3%, and in cover letters, at 25%. They were also worse at spotting it than they thought: given four resumes, 33.5% picked the AI-written one, against a 25% chance baseline. Detection is the smaller risk. The larger one is a page that reads like everyone else's and a bullet you cannot defend.

Tech employers can be more relaxed. [Tech leaders quoted by IEEE-USA InSight had no problem with AI help on a resume or cover letter as long as the content stays accurate](https://insight.ieeeusa.org/articles/what-tech-hiring-managers-really-think-of-ai-created-resumes-and-cover-letters/).

Employers set their own rules. [Anthropic's candidate guidance, updated in July 2025, asks applicants to write their first draft themselves, then use Claude to refine it](https://www.anthropic.com/candidate-ai-guidance). It allows no AI in take-home tests or live interviews unless the company says otherwise. Read each posting's rules and follow them. Assume a take-home or an interview bans it unless the posting says otherwise.

Career centers draw the same line. [Southern Connecticut State University's career center warns](https://southernct.edu/career/ChatGPT-AI-Usage-in-Your-Career-Search) that submitting AI-generated content as your own work, even after you edit it, carries ethical and legal risks.

## Where students stand

In [NACE's 2025 student survey](https://naceweb.org/job-market/trends-and-predictions/student-concerns-about-ai-tempering-their-use-of-it-in-job-search), 67.1% of graduating seniors said they did not use AI in their job search, and the most common reason was concern about the ethics of it, at 28.9%. [A year later, 46% of graduating seniors said they used it](https://naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/ready-or-reluctant-employer-expectations-for-ai-skills-meet-student-skepticism). The survey reached more than 17,000 students at 258 schools, and more than 1,860 of them were graduating seniors. The question now is which jobs to hand the tools.

## Rehearsing interviews

Ask the tool to play the interviewer. Give it one resume line and have it go five layers deep, with the questions from the [[How to interview#Your projects, line by line|Your projects, line by line]] section on the How to interview page: how did you do that, why that way, what broke, what would you change. Answer out loud, at a whiteboard if you have one. Ask it to explain a fundamental you are shaky on, such as what `volatile` does, and then check the answer against [Nigel Jones's article on volatile](https://barrgroup.com/embedded-systems/how-to/c-volatile-keyword).

A chatbot is a rehearsal, not an audience. [In a 2020 study, having an interviewer watch cut performance by more than half](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10196170), and a text box does not do that to you. Book a mock interview at your career center as well. [Illinois Engineering Career Services calls it the best way to prepare](https://ecs.grainger.illinois.edu/student-resources/interviewing/mock).

## Rules for using a tool

- Use it to find, sort, and check. Write every line yourself.
- Never add a tool, a number, or a result it suggests unless you did the work and can defend it at a whiteboard.
- Follow the posting's rule on AI. Assume take-homes and interviews ban it.
- Measure every [X] yourself. A tool cannot read your oscilloscope.
- Read the final page aloud. If you would not say a line to the interviewer, do not send it.

This page is for a reader who is new to electrical engineering, or in the first two years of a degree. It gives a clear order of what to learn and do before you apply to a master's or PhD. If you are still deciding whether a degree is worth it, read the [[Graduate study|graduate study page]] first. The [[Forever learning|forever learning page]] has the free courses this path starts from.

## Pick the degree before the school

MEng, MS, and PhD programs are different degrees. Choose the one that matches the work you want, then pick schools.

An MEng is a course-based professional degree. [Cornell's Electrical and Computer Engineering M.Eng. is 30 credits over two semesters](https://catalog.cornell.edu/programs/electrical-computer-engineering-meng/). [It ends in a faculty-supervised design project and a final report](https://catalog.cornell.edu/programs/electrical-computer-engineering-meng/), not a thesis.

An MS can be a thesis MSECE, a professional master's, a course-only track, or an online track at the same school. [Purdue ECE's master's handbook lists all four, plus a 4+1 BS/MS for its own undergraduates](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/Academics/Graduates/MASTERS/MSECE_Handbook_Print_Fall2025.pdf).

[A PhD does not require a master's first](https://ece.rutgers.edu/graduate-faqs).

The [[Graduate study#Which fields expect one|section on which fields expect one]] on the graduate study page can help you match the degree to the work. So can the [[Career paths|career paths page]].

## Build the foundation

Graduate courses build on circuits, signals and systems, computation structures, and graduate-level math. Fill those gaps before you apply, not after.

The three MIT OpenCourseWare courses in the [[Forever learning#Take free courses|free courses]] section on the forever learning page cover that core. Use those courses to fill gaps.

[Purdue's online MSECE requires 6 credits of graduate math](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/Academics/Graduates/MASTERS/online-track-degree-requirements), and [recommends linear algebra first if your background is thin](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/Academics/Graduates/MASTERS/online-track-degree-requirements).

Pick one field from the [[Career paths|career paths page]] and take its upper-level electives. [Purdue ECE describes a master's as a chance to go deeper into one technical specialty](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/Academics/Graduates/MASTERS/why-masters-degree).

## Get research experience early

PhD applications ask you to show research you have already done. Start with a lab seat or a design team in the first two years.

[MIT EECS asks for an essay on Research Activities and Goals, and for three letters of recommendation](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/graduate-programs/admission-process/graduate-admissions-faqs/).

The [[Hardware#Teams and undergraduate research|teams and undergraduate research]] section on the hardware page covers how to get a lab seat or a design-team role. It also shows how that work reads on a resume. The [[First internship|first internship page]] has the paragraph on NSF REU and DOE SULI programs, including the 2026 caveat.

Alex Doe is the example student. They list Undergraduate Research Assistant, [University lab], Summer 2025, on the [[Hardware#Teams and undergraduate research|hardware]] and [[First internship|first internship]] pages. One summer like that becomes a resume line and a letter writer. The [[Resume|resume page]] shows how to write the line, and the [[Projects|projects page]] covers how a build or a lab seat turns into a line you can defend.

## Take graduate courses as an undergraduate

One or two graduate courses in senior year test whether you like the work, and they can count toward the master's.

[At Georgia Tech, a senior with at least a 2.7 GPA can take a graduate course with department permission](https://catalog.gatech.edu/academics/undergraduate/undergraduate-students-taking-graduate-courses/). [Up to 12 of those hours can count toward a master's if they were not already used for the bachelor's](https://catalog.gatech.edu/academics/undergraduate/undergraduate-students-taking-graduate-courses/).

[Georgia Tech ECE's joint BS/MS lets students apply from sophomore year, waives the GRE and the application fee, and double-counts up to 6 hours](https://ece.gatech.edu/academics/joint-bs-ms). [Qualified students may get graduate or teaching assistantships once they enter the master's](https://ece.gatech.edu/academics/joint-bs-ms).

Ask your own department for its BS/MS or 4+1 rules. The accelerated route in the [[Graduate study#How students pay|how students pay]] section on the graduate study page is the cheap one.

## Find research fit and an advisor

For a PhD, the advisor and their funding often matter as much as the school. Read the lab's recent work before you write to anyone.

[Minnesota ECE recommends that PhD applicants contact faculty, and says advisor funding is a significant factor in admissions](https://cse.umn.edu/ece/program-and-admission-faqs). [Applicants do not have to have an advisor lined up first](https://cse.umn.edu/ece/program-and-admission-faqs).

[Rutgers ECE says you need an advisor within two years, and suggests reading faculty work on Google Scholar first](https://ece.rutgers.edu/graduate-faqs).

[MIT EECS faculty do not discuss funding or supervision until after admission](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/graduate-programs/admission-process/graduate-admissions-faqs/), so check each program's rule before you email.

For a short, specific first message, use the [[Networking#The informational interview|informational interview]] section on the networking page.

## Funded or not, and how assistantships and fellowships work

A PhD must be funded. A master's usually is not, unless an assistantship, a fellowship, or an employer pays.

[Teaching and graduate assistantships and fellowships pay a stipend plus tuition remission, and faculty recommendations play a key role in TA and GA assignments](https://ece.rutgers.edu/graduate-faqs).

[The NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program is one national fellowship that pays a stipend and covers tuition for research-based master's or doctoral study](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/grfp-nsf-graduate-research-fellowship-program/nsf26-526/solicitation). [It provides three years of support, used over a five-year period](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/grfp-nsf-graduate-research-fellowship-program/nsf26-526/solicitation). Read the [program's full eligibility rules](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/opportunities/grfp-nsf-graduate-research-fellowship-program/nsf26-526/solicitation) before you plan around it. The [NSF GRFP FAQ](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/information/faq-additional-faq-nsf-graduate-research-fellowship-program) is a second place to check. Ask your department about other fellowships too.

What a funded PhD pays, and the funding figure, stay on the [[Graduate study#How students pay|how students pay]] section of the graduate study page.

## The application: statement and letters

The statement shows research interest and evidence. The letters come from people who saw that work.

[MIT EECS asks for two essays: one on Research Activities and Goals, and one on the Academic and Intellectual Journey](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/graduate-programs/admission-process/graduate-admissions-faqs/). [It also asks for three letters, and the deadline is December 1](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/graduate-programs/admission-process/graduate-admissions-faqs/). [It does not use GRE scores](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/graduate-programs/admission-process/graduate-admissions-faqs/).

[If you have been working, MIT still asks for at least one faculty letter, with the rest from employers](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/graduate-programs/admission-process/graduate-admissions-faqs/).

[Georgia Tech ECE's BS/MS statement asks for your interests, how the degree supports them, and a recap of accomplishments](https://ece.gatech.edu/graduate/handbook/Information-for-Undergraduate-and-Non-ECE-Students). A short version of those three parts works as a starting structure at other schools.

For the GRE and for essay-writing guides, see the [[Graduate study#The GRE|GRE]] section on the graduate study page. Start a CV from the [[Resume|resume]] page, then add research, papers, and talks. The [[Resume#A CV for a graduate application|CV section]] there covers where a CV differs from a job resume: the overall GPA with its scale, Skills right under Education, and a Teaching heading for grading or mentoring work.

## Study while you work

Some fields do not expect a graduate degree. If you already work in one of them, a part-time or online master's that an employer pays for is often the cheap route. The [[Graduate study#Which fields expect one|section on which fields expect one]] on the graduate study page covers that case.

[Purdue's online MSECE is the same degree as the on-campus program](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/Academics/Online/questions). [It is 30 credits](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/Academics/Online). [Most students who work full time take one 3-credit course a semester, with up to six years to finish](https://engineering.purdue.edu/ECE/Academics/Graduates/MASTERS/online-track-degree-requirements).

Not every program allows this. [MIT EECS degree programs are full-time and on campus only](https://www.eecs.mit.edu/academics/graduate-programs/admission-process/graduate-admissions-faqs/).

For employer tuition, see the [[Forever learning#Employer tuition help|employer tuition help]] section on the forever learning page, and the [[Graduate study#How students pay|how students pay]] section on the graduate study page. Ask about tuition help before you accept an offer. The [[First full-time role|first full-time role page]] covers how to read an offer.

## Checklist

Work through these in order.

- Pick the degree that matches the work you want, MEng, MS, or PhD, then pick schools.
- Fill gaps in circuits, signals and systems, computation structures, and graduate-level math, using the [[Forever learning#Take free courses|free courses]] on the forever learning page.
- Get a lab seat or a design-team role in the first two years, and write it as a resume line.
- Take one or two graduate courses as a senior, and ask your department whether they count toward a master's or a BS/MS.
- For a PhD, read a lab's recent work, then follow that program's rules about contacting faculty.
- Confirm funding. A PhD must be funded, and a master's needs an assistantship, a fellowship, or an employer.
- Write a statement with research interest and evidence, and ask for letters from people who saw the work.
- If you will work first, ask about employer tuition help before you accept, and check whether a program is offered part time or online.

Join IEEE as a student and go to the branch meetings. If SWE, NSBE, or SHPE fits you, join for the convention career fair alone. Accept an honor society invitation if you get one. Decide again at the first full-price renewal after graduation.
I'm a member of IEEE and its Young Professionals group, and I was inducted into IEEE-HKN in 2026. What follows is what each one gave me or a chapter near me, and what working engineers say about keeping it up.

## IEEE

[IEEE student membership](https://www.ieee.org/membership/join) is open while you carry at least half of a full-time load in an accredited program.

Here is what student membership buys. Spectrum and Potentials show up as magazines, and Potentials is the one written for students. Member rates at conferences are where membership pays off, if you go to one. The branch on campus is where the talks and the company visits happen, and it is the one benefit that only works if you show up. [IEEEXtreme, the 24-hour programming contest, drew 19,916 participants in 8,793 teams in 2024](https://2024.ieeeannualreport.org/empowering-the-next-generation/), and gives you a team line for the resume and three teammates who build things.

What it does not buy is IEEE Xplore. Basic membership gives discounts on single papers, and your university library already has the full subscription, so read the papers while you are a student and download the ones you will want later.

After graduation the rate goes up, and working engineers split on whether to keep paying. [On one r/ElectricalEngineering thread from April 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/ElectricalEngineering/comments/1jukzwd/should_i_join_the_ieee/), one member said the conference discounts were worth more than the membership fee, and another called it worth it as a student. [On an r/ECE thread from March 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/ECE/comments/1j4yzv6/how_do_i_benefit_the_most_from_an_ieee_membership/), an engineer with about 25 years in the field said it is really not worth it unless you are going to multiple conferences. I kept mine after graduation and joined the Young Professionals group, which runs the events for people in their first years of work. Whether it stays worth it for me comes down to the same test: the conferences. If your work does not go to them, the two engineers above are right, and the full-price renewal is where to stop.

IEEE-USA's salary calculator is the member tool that matters for the job hunt. [IEEE Young Professionals explains that members who take the yearly IEEE-USA salary survey can use the calculator at no cost](https://yp.ieee.org/blog/2023/11/15/check-out-the-career-programs-of-ieee-usa-part-2). Use it next to the pay sources on the [[Help#Pay and offers|Pay and offers]] section on the Help page.

## IEEE-HKN and Tau Beta Pi

Both are honor societies. You join by invitation, from grades, and the membership is for life. HKN inducted me in 2026, the year I graduated, so the invitation can come late, and it still counts.

[IEEE-HKN induction includes IEEE student membership for the calendar year you are inducted](https://hkn.ieee.org/knowledge-base/what-is-the-cost-of-an-induction?seq_no=2). [HKN says employers generally have a close recruiting relationship with chapters](https://hkn.ieee.org/about/benefits). That is HKN describing itself, so treat it as a reason to go to the chapter's events, not as a promise.

Tau Beta Pi is the all-engineering honor society. You are initiated once, through your chapter, and there are no annual dues. The value is in the awards: [in 2025, Tau Beta Pi gave 254 senior-year scholarships of \$2,000 from 532 applicants](https://www.tbp.org/static/docs/ScholarInfoSheet26.pdf), and [31 graduate fellowships of \$10,000 from 376 applicants](https://www.tbp.org/static/docs/FellowshipInfoSheet26.pdf). Those are better odds than most scholarships you will find.

## SWE, NSBE, and SHPE

Each runs a yearly convention with a career fair bigger than any campus fair.
- [SWE has a collegiate membership](https://swe.org/membership/collegiate-membership/), and [one option covers college plus the first professional year](https://swe.org/membership). [The WE25 career fair in October 2025 had hundreds of organizations](https://we25.swe.org/schedule-highlight/career-fair/), and the floor opened to members first.
- [NSBE has a collegiate membership](https://www.nsbe.org/membership/graduate-students-membership), and chapters may add their own. [More than 15,600 people attended the 2025 convention in Chicago](https://annualreport25.nsbe.org/events/), and [the Department of Energy expected 400-plus career fair exhibitors](https://www.energy.gov/node/4848689).
- [SHPE has undergraduate and graduate memberships](https://shpe.org/membership/become-a-member/). [The 2025 convention in Philadelphia expected 12,000 attendees and more than 150 companies recruiting, interviewing, and hiring on site](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/education/2025-shpe-national-convention-brings-12-000-attendees-to-philadelphia-for-a-stem-career-f-1070160). [SHPE and SWE members get a discount when they join the other](https://shpe.org/membership/become-a-member/).

Treat the convention like a campus fair with ten times the booths. The [[Getting hired#Cold outreach and career fairs|Cold outreach and career fairs]] section on the Getting hired page has the card to prepare, and the [[Networking#Career fairs|Career fairs]] section on the Networking page has what the odds look like at one booth.

## SPIE, Optica, and ACM

For photonics and optics, [SPIE student membership](https://spie.org/membership/member-benefits) comes with a journal and reduced conference registration, and Optica has a student membership too. For the software side, [ACM has student membership, with or without the Digital Library](https://www.acm.org/membership/membership-options). Join the one whose conference your field actually goes to.

## On the resume

List a membership only with a role or a result: branch officer, competition team, a paper, a scholarship. A bare line that says "IEEE member" tells a screener nothing. Date it. The [[Resume#Skills|Skills]] section on the Resume page covers where the line goes.

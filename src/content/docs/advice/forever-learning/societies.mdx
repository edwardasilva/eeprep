Join IEEE as a student and go to the branch meetings. If SWE, NSBE, or SHPE fits you, join for the convention career fair alone. Accept an honor society invitation if you get one. Decide again at the first full-price renewal after graduation. The whole set costs about \$30 to \$60 a year as a student, depending on which ones you join and where you live, which is less than one textbook.

I'm a member of IEEE and its Young Professionals group, and I was inducted into IEEE-HKN in 2026. What follows is what each one cost me or a chapter near me, what I got, and what working engineers say about keeping it up.

## IEEE

Student dues run about \$30 to \$40 a year, depending on the region you live in. [Auburn's student branch lists the US rate at \$32](https://ieee.auburn.edu/?p=34), and [IEEE's dues page](https://www.ieee.org/membership/join/dues.html) has this year's number for your region. You qualify while you carry at least half of a full-time load in an accredited program.

I joined in my last year of school. Here is what the student rate buys. Spectrum and Potentials show up as magazines, and Potentials is the one written for students. Member rates at conferences are where the dues come back, if you go to one. The branch on campus is where the talks and the company visits happen, and it is the one benefit that only works if you show up. [IEEEXtreme, the 24-hour programming contest, is free with student membership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IEEEXtreme), drew more than 19,500 participants in 2024, and gives you a team line for the resume and three teammates who build things.

What it does not buy is IEEE Xplore. Basic membership gives discounts on single papers, and your university library already has the full subscription, so read the papers while you are a student and download the ones you will want later.

After graduation the dues go up by a lot, and working engineers split on whether to keep paying. [On one r/ElectricalEngineering thread from April 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/ElectricalEngineering/comments/1jukzwd/should_i_join_the_ieee/), one member said the conference discounts were worth more than the membership fee, and another called it worth it as a student at the discounted rate. [On an r/ECE thread from March 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/ECE/comments/1j4yzv6/how_do_i_benefit_the_most_from_an_ieee_membership/), an engineer with about 25 years in the field said it is really not worth it unless you are going to multiple conferences. I kept mine after graduation and joined the Young Professionals group, which runs the events for people in their first years of work. Whether it stays worth it for me comes down to the same test: the conferences. If your work does not go to them, the two engineers above are right, and the full-price renewal is where to stop.

IEEE-USA's salary calculator is the member tool that matters for the job hunt. [IEEE Young Professionals explains that members who take the yearly IEEE-USA salary survey can use the calculator at no cost](https://yp.ieee.org/blog/2023/11/15/check-out-the-career-programs-of-ieee-usa-part-2). Use it next to the pay sources on the [[Help#Pay and offers|Pay and offers]] section on the Help page.

## IEEE-HKN and Tau Beta Pi

Both are honor societies. You join by invitation, from grades, and the membership is for life. HKN inducted me in 2026, the year I graduated, so the invitation can come late, and it still counts.

[IEEE-HKN charges a one-time \$78 for inductions on or after 16 August 2023, and that fee includes one year of IEEE student membership](https://hkn.ieee.org/knowledge-base/what-is-the-cost-of-an-induction?seq_no=2). [HKN says employers generally have a close recruiting relationship with chapters](https://hkn.ieee.org/about/benefits). That is HKN describing itself, so treat it as a reason to go to the chapter's events, not as a promise.

Tau Beta Pi is the all-engineering honor society. The one-time fee runs about \$75 to \$110, depending on the chapter: [Carnegie Mellon's charges \$75](https://www.andrew.cmu.edu/user/tbp/initiation.html) and [UC Irvine's charges \$110](https://tbp.eng.uci.edu/content/initiation-requirements). There are no annual dues. The money is in the awards: [in 2025, Tau Beta Pi gave 254 senior-year scholarships of \$2,000 from 532 applicants](https://www.tbp.org/static/docs/ScholarInfoSheet26.pdf), and [31 graduate fellowships of \$10,000 from 376 applicants](https://www.tbp.org/static/docs/FellowshipInfoSheet26.pdf). Those are better odds than most scholarships you will find.

## SWE, NSBE, and SHPE

Each runs a yearly convention with a career fair bigger than any campus fair. The dues are about \$10 to \$20 a year, plus whatever your chapter adds.

- [SWE collegiate membership starts at \$20 a year](https://swe.org/membership/collegiate-membership/), and [a one-time \$50 covers college plus the first professional year](https://swe.org/membership). [The WE25 career fair in October 2025 had hundreds of organizations](https://we25.swe.org/schedule-highlight/career-fair/), and the floor opened to members first.
- [NSBE collegiate membership is \$15 a year](https://www.nsbe.org/membership/graduate-students-membership), plus chapter dues. [More than 15,600 people attended the 2025 convention in Chicago](https://annualreport25.nsbe.org/events/), and [the Department of Energy expected 400-plus career fair exhibitors](https://www.energy.gov/node/4848689).
- SHPE undergraduate dues are about \$10 a year on [chapter pages](https://shpe.umbc.edu/membership/types/). [The 2025 convention in Philadelphia expected 12,000 attendees and more than 150 companies recruiting, interviewing, and hiring on site](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/education/2025-shpe-national-convention-brings-12-000-attendees-to-philadelphia-for-a-stem-career-f-1070160). [SHPE and SWE give each other's members 25 percent off dues](https://shpe.org/membership/become-a-member/member-faqs/).

Treat the convention like a campus fair with ten times the booths. The [[Getting hired#Cold outreach and career fairs|Cold outreach and career fairs]] section on the Getting hired page has the card to prepare, and the [[Networking#Career fairs|Career fairs]] section on the Networking page has what the odds look like at one booth.

## SPIE, Optica, and ACM

For photonics and optics, [SPIE student membership is \$20 a year](https://spie.org/membership/member-benefits) and comes with a journal and reduced conference registration. Optica's student rate is about \$20 on [chapter pages](https://creol.ucf.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/2/2024/10/OPTICA-Membership-Dues-Guidline.pdf.pdf). For the software side, [ACM student membership is \$19 a year, or \$42 with the Digital Library](https://www.acm.org/membership/membership-options). Join the one whose conference your field actually goes to.

## On the resume

List a membership only with a role or a result: branch officer, competition team, a paper, a scholarship. A bare line that says "IEEE member" tells a screener nothing. Date it. The [[Resume#Skills|Skills]] section on the Resume page covers where the line goes.

Join IEEE as a student and go to the branch meetings. If you are already in your final year, join only if you will go to branch events or a conference before you graduate. The Society of Women Engineers (SWE), the National Society of Black Engineers (NSBE), and the Society of Hispanic Professional Engineers (SHPE) each run a convention career fair. If one fits you, the fair alone is reason enough to join. Accept an honor society invitation if you get one. At the first full-price renewal after graduation, decide again whether each membership is worth paying for.

## IEEE

[IEEE student membership](https://www.ieee.org/membership/qualifications) is open while you carry at least half of a normal full-time load as a registered student in an IEEE-designated field.

Student membership brings two magazines, Spectrum and Potentials, and Potentials is the one written for students. Member rates at conferences are where membership pays off, if you go to one. The branch on campus is where the talks and the company visits happen, and it is the one benefit that only works if you show up. [IEEEXtreme, the 24-hour programming contest, drew 19,916 participants in 8,793 teams in 2024](https://2024.ieeeannualreport.org/empowering-the-next-generation/). It gives you a team line for the resume and up to two teammates who build things.

Membership does not include IEEE Xplore, IEEE's library of papers, but membership alone gives discounts on single papers. Your university library may subscribe, so check there before you pay for one. If it does, read the papers while you are a student, and download the ones you will want later.

After graduation the rate goes up. IEEE's Young Professionals group runs events for members in their first years of work, but working engineers split on whether to keep paying. [On one r/ElectricalEngineering thread from April 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/ElectricalEngineering/comments/1jukzwd/should_i_join_the_ieee/), one member called it worth it as a student. [On an r/ECE thread from March 2025](https://www.reddit.com/r/ECE/comments/1j4yzv6/how_do_i_benefit_the_most_from_an_ieee_membership/), an engineer with about 25 years in the field said the benefit is near zero. A different commenter said it is not worth it unless you go to multiple conferences. Whether membership stays worth it comes down to the same test as for a student, which is whether you go to conferences. If your job does not take you to conferences, those commenters are right, and the full-price renewal is where to stop.

IEEE-USA's salary calculator is the member tool that matters for the job hunt. [IEEE-USA InSight says young professionals who take the yearly IEEE-USA salary survey can use the calculator free of charge](https://insight.ieeeusa.org/articles/more-ieee-usa-career-and-membership-services-programs-part-2/). Once you start work, use it next to the pay sources in the [[Resources#Pay|pay]] section on the resources page.

## IEEE-HKN and Tau Beta Pi

IEEE-HKN (Eta Kappa Nu) and Tau Beta Pi are both honor societies. You join by invitation, based on your grades, and the membership is for life. The invitation can come as late as your final year, and it still counts.

[IEEE-HKN induction includes IEEE student membership for the calendar year you are inducted](https://hkn.ieee.org/knowledge-base/what-is-the-cost-of-an-induction?seq_no=2). [HKN says employers generally have a close recruiting relationship with chapters](https://hkn.ieee.org/about/benefits). That is HKN describing itself, so treat it as a reason to go to the chapter's events, not as a promise.

Tau Beta Pi is the all-engineering honor society. You are initiated once, through your chapter, and there are no annual dues. The value is in the awards. [In 2025, Tau Beta Pi gave 254 senior-year scholarships of up to \$2,000 from 532 applicants](https://www.tbp.org/static/docs/ScholarInfoSheet26.pdf), and [31 graduate fellowships of \$10,000 from 376 applicants](https://www.tbp.org/static/docs/FellowshipInfoSheet26.pdf). The senior-year scholarships went to nearly half of the applicants.

## SWE, NSBE, and SHPE

Each runs a yearly convention with a large career fair.

- [SWE has a collegiate membership, and one option covers college plus the first professional year](https://swe.org/membership/collegiate-membership/). [The WE25 career fair in October 2025 had hundreds of organizations](https://we25.swe.org/schedule-highlight/career-fair/).
- [NSBE has a collegiate membership](https://nsbe.org/collegiate/), through a campus chapter or at large. [More than 15,600 people attended the March 2025 convention in Chicago](https://annualreport25.nsbe.org/events/), and [the Department of Energy expected 400-plus career fair exhibitors](https://www.energy.gov/node/4848689).
- [SHPE has undergraduate and graduate memberships](https://shpe.org/membership/become-a-member/). [The 2025 convention in Philadelphia, from October 29 to November 1, expected 12,000 attendees and more than 150 companies recruiting, interviewing, and hiring on site](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/education/2025-shpe-national-convention-brings-12-000-attendees-to-philadelphia-for-a-stem-career-f-1070160). [SHPE and SWE members get a discount when they join the other](https://shpe.org/membership/become-a-member/).

Prepare for a convention fair the way you would for a campus fair. The [[Getting hired#Cold outreach and career fairs|cold outreach and career fairs]] section on the getting hired page has the card to prepare. The [[Networking#Career fairs|career fairs]] section on the networking page shows the odds at one booth.

## SPIE, Optica, and ACM

For photonics and optics, [SPIE has a student membership and lists a free journal subscription among its member benefits](https://spie.org/membership/member-benefits), and [Optica has a student membership too](https://www.optica.org/get_involved/students/). For the software side, [the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM) has student membership, with or without the Digital Library](https://www.acm.org/membership/membership-options). Join the society whose conference the people in your field attend.

## On the resume

List a membership only with a role or a result, such as branch officer, a competition team, a paper, or a scholarship, and give it dates. A bare line that says "IEEE member" tells a screener nothing. The [[Resume#Education|education section]] on the resume page puts memberships on the "Activities:" line, and its [[Resume#Projects|projects section]] says where technical club work goes instead.

eeprep is written by an electrical engineering graduate who now writes embedded software. They started as a computer science major. Circuits and digital logic classes showed them the hardware side, they liked it more than they expected, and they switched to electrical engineering.

The advice came out of what followed: a six-month co-op that showed which work did not fit, an internship in real-time signal processing that showed which work did, and a fall of applications that went nowhere until the method changed. The interviews ran September–November of senior year. They graduated in May 2026 and started work as an embedded software engineer in July 2026.

## What that path taught

Each lesson has its own page on the site.

- A finished project you can defend opens the first door. The [[Projects|Projects page]] covers it.
- One clean page in a standard template gets read. The [[Resume|Resume page]] covers it.
- If 20 applications bring zero interviews, change the resume or the channel. The [[Getting hired|Getting hired page]] covers it.
- A referral beats a cold application. The [[Networking|Networking page]] covers it.
- Interviewers go as deep as you let them, so know every line and never bluff. The [[How to interview|How to interview page]] covers it.

They started eeprep because people kept asking for help with their resumes, and it is where they wrote down the system that worked. Next they want to study at the graduate level and, in time, teach.

Everything else on the site comes from a linked source. Where a page gives the author's own view, it says so.

The market is crowded at the entry level and fine in the long run. More people apply to every posting, there are fewer postings for new graduates, and the demand for electrical engineers over the next decade is still growing. That mix changes how you apply, not whether the degree pays. Aim at a person, not a pile.

<MarketFigure />

## More applications per posting

[Greenhouse, which runs hiring for thousands of employers, said in June 2025 that applications per job on its platform had risen 102 percent since ChatGPT's release](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-launches-dream-job-to-fix-job-hunting-and-boost-candidates-chances-of-getting-hired). [Workday counted 173 million applications for 19 million requisitions in the first half of 2024](https://newsroom.workday.com/2024-09-10-Workday-Global-Workforce-Report-Job-Market-Tightens-as-AI-Reshapes-Hiring-Processes), with applications growing four times faster than openings. [Ashby's data on 38 million applications](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals) shows where that leaves the person who applies online: the offer rate for inbound applicants fell from 7 in 1,000 to 2 in 1,000 between 2021 and 2024.

A pile that size is why the referral and the career fair come first. The [[Networking|networking page]] has the numbers for both.

## Fewer postings for new graduates

[Handshake's class of 2025 report said job postings on its platform were down 15 percent over the past year, while applications per job had risen 30 percent](https://joinhandshake.com/network-trends/class-of-2025-graduation/). The average student had [sent 16.5 applications on Handshake by late April 2025, 21 percent more than the class of 2024](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/180526). [Indeed's tech talent report put tech postings in July 2025 at 36 percent below early 2020](https://d341ezm4iqaae0.cloudfront.net/ews/20250925161702/Indeed-Tech-Talent-Report-2025-US.pdf).
[Indeed Hiring Lab found that bachelor's graduates creating or updating Indeed profiles in their graduation year rose 67 percent from 2023 to 2025](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/04/23/new-grads-looking-for-work-the-struggle-is-real/). The same report puts the unemployment rate for recent graduates at 5.7 percent in the fourth quarter of 2025, a three-year high.

Those are tech-wide figures. Hardware is a smaller slice of them, and the hardware postings follow their own calendar. The [[First internship#Recruiting calendar|recruiting calendar]] section on the First internship page and the [[First full-time role#Recruiting timeline|recruiting timeline]] section on the First full-time role page have the months.

## Still, the degree holds

[The BLS projects 8 percent growth for electrical and electronics engineers from 2025 to 2035, with about 16,300 openings a year over the decade](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/electrical-and-electronics-engineers.htm). [NACE's 2026 student survey found that more than two in five bachelor's graduates had at least one job offer before graduation](https://naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/more-than-two-out-of-five-of-college-class-of-2026-had-a-job-offer-in-hand-by-graduation), and the class averaged 0.79 offers per graduate. The class of 2025 [started its search 6.4 months before graduation](https://www.naceweb.org/job-market/trends-and-predictions/facing-a-tough-job-market-class-of-2025-responded-accordingly), earlier than the class before it.

Hiring is picking up. [In NACE's Job Outlook 2026 Spring Update, employers planned to hire 5.6 percent more graduates from the class of 2026](https://www.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/trends-and-predictions/employers-expect-to-hire-5-point-6-percent-more-new-college-graduates-this-year), with engineering services among the industries planning increases. [The Semiconductor Industry Association projects nearly 115,000 new chip industry jobs by 2030](https://www.semiconductors.org/chipping-away-assessing-and-addressing-the-labor-market-gap-facing-the-u-s-semiconductor-industry/), and estimates that about 67,000 of them risk going unfilled at current degree completion rates. [A November 2026 Semiconductor Engineering panel saw no drop in demand for EE graduates](https://semiengineering.com/college-grads-and-ai/), though AI agents now do many tasks that used to go to junior engineers. To see how your own major is doing, [the New York Fed tracks unemployment, underemployment, and pay for recent graduates by major](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market).

The [[Resources#Pay|pay]] section on the Resources page has the pay data. The BLS median covers every career stage, so do not expect it in a first offer.

## What students see

[One student saw many summer 2027 internship posts and only a few 2027 new-graduate posts in August 2026](https://www.reddit.com/r/ElectricalEngineering/comments/1vvqcgw/lack_of_2027_new_grad_jobs/), and commenters told them to wait for the fall. [A new graduate asking how hard the search is got the answer that it often comes down to finding a company that needs a Level 1 engineer](https://www.reddit.com/r/ElectricalEngineering/comments/1wj9mw3/how_easy_it_is_to_find_a_job_for_a_new_grad_if_my/), and that quality, test, and manufacturing titles raise the odds. The application counts students post are in the [[Job boards#What students report|section on what students report]] on the Job boards page.

## What to do about it

1. Get a referral before you apply. It is the one input that moves the 2 in 1,000.
2. Apply within two weeks of a posting. The [[Getting hired#Where to look|section on where to look]] on the Getting hired page has why.
3. After about 20 quiet applications, reevaluate the resume, the channel, and the roles you target. Then keep going.
4. Search the test, validation, and manufacturing titles. The [[Hardware#Test or technician roles as a way in|section on test or technician roles as a way in]] on the Hardware page has the list.
5. Keep the second wave in view. Spring hiring exists, and so do roles that open after graduation. The [[First full-time role#If nothing lands by graduation|section on what to do if nothing lands by graduation]] on the First full-time role page has that plan.

## Why reevaluate at 20

The numbers above are why the 20-application checkpoint exists. It is a rule of thumb from experience, not a measured cutoff. Treat a quiet channel as a sign to reevaluate the resume, the channel, and the targeting, then keep applying. In theory every resume is tailored to the job. That is unrealistic over dozens of applications, so keep [[Resume#Tailor it to the posting|one version per field]] you apply to, and adjust it for each posting.

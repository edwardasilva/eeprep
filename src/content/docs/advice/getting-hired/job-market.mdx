The market is crowded at the entry level and fine in the long run. More people apply to every posting, there are fewer postings for new graduates, and the demand for electrical engineers over the next decade is still growing. That mix changes how you apply, not whether the degree pays, so put your effort into reaching a person through a referral or a career fair.

<MarketFigure />

## More applications per posting

[Greenhouse, a hiring software company used by thousands of employers, said in June 2025 that applications per job on its platform had risen 102 percent since ChatGPT's release](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/greenhouse-launches-dream-job-to-fix-job-hunting-and-boost-candidates-chances-of-getting-hired). [Workday counted 173 million applications for 19 million job openings in the first half of 2024](https://newsroom.workday.com/2024-09-10-Workday-Global-Workforce-Report-Job-Market-Tightens-as-AI-Reshapes-Hiring-Processes), and applications grew four times faster than openings.

[Ashby's data on 38 million applications](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals) shows what that means for people who apply online on their own. Ashby calls them inbound applicants, and their offer rate fell from 7 in 1,000 to 2 in 1,000 between 2021 and 2024. A pile that size is why the referral and the career fair come first, and the [[Networking|networking page]] has the numbers for both.

## Fewer postings for new graduates

[Handshake's class of 2025 report said job postings on its platform were down 15 percent over the past year, while applications per job had risen 30 percent](https://joinhandshake.com/network-trends/class-of-2025-graduation/). [Indeed's tech talent report put tech postings in July 2025 at 36 percent below early 2020](https://d341ezm4iqaae0.cloudfront.net/ews/20250925161702/Indeed-Tech-Talent-Report-2025-US.pdf).

Students also send more applications than before, and more new graduates are looking for work on Indeed. The average student had [sent 16.5 applications on Handshake by late April 2025, 21 percent more than the class of 2024](https://www.insidehighered.com/node/180526). [Indeed Hiring Lab found that the number of bachelor's graduates who created or updated Indeed profiles in their graduation year rose 67 percent from 2023 to 2025](https://hiringlab.indeed.com/2026/04/23/new-grads-looking-for-work-the-struggle-is-real/). The same Hiring Lab report puts the unemployment rate for recent graduates at 5.7 percent in the fourth quarter of 2025, a three-year high.

Those figures cover tech or the whole job market, not hardware alone. Hardware is a smaller slice, and its postings follow their own calendar. The [[First internship#Recruiting calendar|recruiting calendar]] section on the first internship page and the [[First full-time role#Recruiting timeline|recruiting timeline]] section on the first full-time role page have the months.

## Still, the degree holds

[The Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) projects 8 percent growth for electrical and electronics engineers from 2025 to 2035, with about 16,300 openings a year over the decade](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/electrical-and-electronics-engineers.htm). [The 2026 student survey by the National Association of Colleges and Employers (NACE) found that more than two in five bachelor's graduates had at least one job offer before graduation](https://naceweb.org/about-us/press/2026/more-than-two-out-of-five-of-college-class-of-2026-had-a-job-offer-in-hand-by-graduation). The class of 2026 averaged 0.79 offers per graduate. The class of 2025 [started its search 6.4 months before graduation](https://www.naceweb.org/job-market/trends-and-predictions/facing-a-tough-job-market-class-of-2025-responded-accordingly), earlier than the class before it.

[In NACE's Job Outlook 2026 Spring Update, employers surveyed in spring 2026 planned to hire 5.6 percent more graduates from the class of 2026](https://www.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/trends-and-predictions/employers-expect-to-hire-5-point-6-percent-more-new-college-graduates-this-year). Engineering services was among the industries planning increases. That forecast is a plan, not a count of hires. It came after unemployment for recent graduates hit 5.7 percent in the fourth quarter of 2025, the three-year high noted above.

[In a 2023 report, the Semiconductor Industry Association projected nearly 115,000 new chip industry jobs by 2030](https://www.semiconductors.org/chipping-away-assessing-and-addressing-the-labor-market-gap-facing-the-u-s-semiconductor-industry/), and estimated that about 67,000 of them risk going unfilled at current degree completion rates. [A November 2026 Semiconductor Engineering panel saw no drop in demand for electrical engineering graduates](https://semiengineering.com/college-grads-and-ai/), though AI agents now do many tasks that used to go to junior engineers. [The New York Fed tracks unemployment, underemployment, and pay for recent graduates by major](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market), so you can see how your own major is doing.

The [[Resources#Pay|pay]] section on the resources page has the numbers. The BLS median pay there covers every career stage, so do not expect it in a first offer.

## What students see

[One student saw many summer 2027 internship postings and only a few 2027 new-grad postings in August 2026](https://www.reddit.com/r/ElectricalEngineering/comments/1vvqcgw/lack_of_2027_new_grad_jobs/), and commenters told them to wait for the fall. [When a student asked how hard the new-grad search is, one answer said it takes luck to find a company that needs a Level 1 (entry-level) engineer](https://www.reddit.com/r/ElectricalEngineering/comments/1wj9mw3/how_easy_it_is_to_find_a_job_for_a_new_grad_if_my/). The same answer said quality, test, and manufacturing titles raise the odds. The application counts that students post are in the [[Job boards#What students report|section on what students report]] on the job boards page.

## What to do about it

1. Get a referral before you apply, because the 2-in-1,000 offer rate is for people who apply on their own.
2. Apply within two weeks of a posting. The [[Getting hired#Where to look|section on where to look]] on the getting hired page has the reason.
3. After about 50 applications with zero interviews, reevaluate the resume, the channel you apply through, and the roles you target, then keep going.
4. Search the test, validation, and manufacturing titles. The [[Hardware#Test or technician roles as a way in|section on test or technician roles as a way in]] on the hardware page has the list.
5. Keep the second wave in view: spring hiring exists, and so do roles that open after graduation. The [[First full-time role#If nothing lands by graduation|section on what to do if nothing lands by graduation]] on the first full-time role page has that plan.

## Why reevaluate at 50

The rule of 50 in step 3 above comes from the numbers on this page. [In the same Ashby data, 3 percent of people who applied on their own got an interview](https://www.ashbyhq.com/talent-trends-report/reports/referrals). At that rate, 20 applications bring zero interviews about half the time by luck alone, and 50 still do about one time in five. At the same rate, 30 applications bring about one interview on average. So treat about 50 applications with zero interviews as a sign to reevaluate the resume, the channel you apply through, and the roles you target, then keep going. It is a rule of thumb, not a measured cutoff, and zero interviews from 50 applications does not prove the resume is weak.

Fully tailoring a resume to every job is unrealistic over dozens of applications. Keep a master resume, one file that holds everything you have done. eeprep's rule of thumb is to build [[Resume#Tailor it to the posting|one version for each kind of role]] from it, up to 4 or 5, and send the closest. Tailor by hand only for a posting that matters.

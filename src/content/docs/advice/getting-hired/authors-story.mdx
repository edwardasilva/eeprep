## Referrals

Referrals work. I learned that from the other side. About three months after I started, once people knew my name, I referred several friends. Each one went straight to the top of the resume pile. In my experience, a referral carried weight once the person giving it was known on the team. A referral would have made my own process far easier.

## Interviews

The process was long, tiring, and hard. A recruiter first pitched me for a power electronics role, and the hiring manager moved me to software. [[How to interview#The recruiter moved the file]] tells that part. Then came a second phone screen, a three-hour take-home coding challenge, and a call with that manager.

The on-site day opened with a factory tour that counted as part of the interview. Engineers pointed at equipment and asked how I thought it worked. Then I gave an hour-long talk on my internship work to six engineers, and most of them are now my team. They interrupted constantly to push on the details.

Four one-hour interviews followed, mostly at a whiteboard. They covered system design, my capstone in depth, a coding problem, and a behavioral talk with my manager. One whiteboard session asked how to build a computer from the ground up. There were no debugging questions. Lunch counted too, and the day ran 6 to 7 hours.

Within half an hour of the last interview, I knew I was moving on. Half-hour calls with a director and then a vice president closed it out. [[How to interview#The talk and the board]] has more on the day, and [[How to interview]] walks through how to prepare.

What I took from it is to be confident without being arrogant, and to never bluff. You need to know everything you talk about in as much detail as possible. Explaining and defending my thinking out loud is still most of the job.

## What I'd tell a student now

- Ask for a referral before you apply. [[Getting hired#Get a referral first]] lists who to ask.
- If you can't get one, apply the day the posting opens.
- If 20 applications bring zero interviews, change the resume or the channel.
- Show a project that isn't finished if the progress is real.
- Know every line of your resume in full detail, because interviewers will ask.
- Practice LeetCode-style problems out loud at a whiteboard.
- Be confident, and never bluff.

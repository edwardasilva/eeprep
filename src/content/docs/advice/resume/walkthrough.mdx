This page follows one made-up student, Alex Doe, from a first draft that a screener would skip. Each step names what to edit and why. The end product is the one-page PDF shown in the [[Resume#Alex's finished page|section on Alex's finished page]] on the resume page, built from a data file you can copy and fill in.

Projects earn the first interview, because they are what a student has before anyone has paid them to do engineering. Once you have an internship, a co-op, or other technical work, Experience moves above Projects and takes the most room. A reader wants to know what value you added somewhere, and a paid role answers that in a way a class project cannot. Alex's page below is a projects page because Alex has no internship or other technical work yet. The section on when experience takes over shows what happens once that changes.

<DoorFigure />

## TLDR;

- List every class lab, club build, repair, and job before you open a template.
- Until you have an internship or other technical work, order the sections Education, Projects, Experience, Skills.
- Write every bullet as what you did, the tool you used, and the result, and measure each bracketed number instead of guessing it.
- Leave empty space alone in your first year, then fill it with another lab or club build and keep the spacing within the layout limits.
- Once you have an internship or other technical work, move Experience above Projects, give it your biggest numbers, and keep only the projects you can still defend.

## Start with an inventory

Before you touch a template, list everything: class labs where you built or measured something, club builds, repairs, and every job. This list is the start of your master resume, the one file that holds everything you have done. The [[Workshop#Project inventory for students with no experience|section on the project inventory for students with no experience]] on the workshop page has the form. Alex's list came to two builds (a CubeSat power board and a bench data logger), a retail job, three upper-level courses, a club, and an honor society. That is enough for one page.

## The first draft

Alex's first draft reads like most first drafts. It opens with an objective, leads with the retail job, says "worked on" and "utilized", and lists teamwork as a skill.

<DraftFigure />

1. Cut the objective. [The r/EngineeringResumes wiki keeps an objective or summary only for senior engineers, career changers, and people returning from a gap](https://www.reddit.com/r/EngineeringResumes/wiki/index/). If you keep one, make it one line that names the role you want.
2. Move Projects above Experience. Alex has no internship or other technical work yet, so the order is Education, Projects, Experience, Skills. That is [the wiki's order for a student with no technical work experience](https://www.reddit.com/r/EngineeringResumes/wiki/index/). The retail job stays, with its two bullets, for the same reason.
3. Rewrite every bullet as did X with Y to get Z. "Worked on a buck converter for a CubeSat using LTspice and KiCad" becomes "Designed a [X] W CubeSat buck converter in KiCad and LTspice, measured at [X]% efficiency on an electronic load". The [[Resume#Bullets|bullets]] section on the resume page shows the pattern. The [[Hardware#Hardware builds that count|hardware builds that count]] section on the hardware page has longer lines for the same kind of build.
4. Put the dates at the right margin, with one style in each section. Alex dates the retail job by month and the projects by semester, because each project ran less than a year. That is eeprep's rule: jobs by month, and projects by semester unless one runs longer than a year, which moves the whole Projects section to months. [The wiki is stricter and says that if you use months, do not mix them with semesters or seasons](https://www.reddit.com/r/EngineeringResumes/wiki/index/). Alex's page follows eeprep's rule instead.
5. Cut the soft skills, Microsoft Office, and the references line. Teamwork shows up in an interview, not in a list, and [every skill that stays needs a bullet behind it](https://ecs.grainger.illinois.edu/student-resources/resumes-letters/resumes).

Every [X] is a number Alex measures on the bench. If you do not have the number yet, measure it. If you can no longer measure it, leave the number out, and never guess.

## The finished page

The result is the finished page figure in the [[Resume#Alex's finished page|section on Alex's finished page]] on the resume page. It has one column, a centered header, Projects above Experience, and two labeled skill lines where every tool appears in a bullet. Download [the finished PDF](https://github.com/edwardasilva/resume-generator/blob/main/examples/Alex-Doe-Resume.pdf) to see the real page.

The page is built from one data file. [resume-generator](https://github.com/edwardasilva/resume-generator) reads it and does the layout, so the content is all you write. Here is the projects block from [Alex's data file](https://github.com/edwardasilva/resume-generator/blob/main/examples/alex.json):

```json title="examples/alex.json" wrap
"projects": [
  {
    "title": "CubeSat Power Board",
    "tools": ["KiCad", "LTspice"],
    "start": "Fall 2025",
    "end": "Spring 2026",
    "bullets": [
      "Designed a [X] W CubeSat buck converter in KiCad and LTspice, measured at [X]% efficiency on an electronic load",
      "Cut output ripple from [X] mV to [X] mV by shrinking the switching loop, verified on an oscilloscope"
    ]
  }
]
```

Unzip the latest release, replace Alex's details in `resume.json` with yours, and run `./run`. It writes `[Name]-Resume.pdf`, fits it onto one page, and checks that the text extracts cleanly. The [[Resume#Build your resume|section on building your resume]] on the resume page has the download steps. If a bullet spills a word or two onto a second line, shorten it to one line or add a detail that fills the second line, then run `./run` again.

## When experience takes over

The same rules produce a different page in your first year and a different one at graduation, and the difference is mostly how much paid work there is to show. resume-generator ships five finished resume examples, all under the Alex Doe name, one for each stage. The senior page is the one this walkthrough and the resume page follow.

Three things change as you go. Education stays at the top through graduation, and an internship or other technical work moves Experience up above Projects, as on the sophomore and new-grad pages. Once you start a full-time job, Experience leads and Education moves to the bottom. Each internship pushes the oldest class project down the list, and eventually off the page. The skill lines grow, but every tool on them still needs a bullet.

Once you have an internship or other technical work, give that work the bullets with the biggest numbers. Keep the one or two projects you can still defend line by line, and let the rest go. When employers choose between two equal candidates, [an internship with them or in their industry weighs most](https://www.naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/candidate-selection/internship-experience-often-the-deciding-factor-between-equal-candidates). The internship is also the work an interview asks you to explain.

The first-year page has empty space at the bottom, which is fine in your first year. Fill it later with content, such as a lab or a club build, and keep the spacing within the limits in the [[Resume#Layout|layout]] section on the resume page.

## What to do this week

Fill in the inventory, pick the two builds you can defend best, and write one XYZ bullet (did X with Y to get Z) for each. Measure anything that still reads [X]. Then run the audit on the [[Cleanup|cleanup page]] before you send the PDF anywhere. If you have an internship, start with its bullets instead, and give them the most room.
